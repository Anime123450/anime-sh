# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue with the code, not the person who wrote it. Assume the other
person is working from information you do not have — on this project that is
usually literally true, because half the surprising behaviour comes from a
provider quirk nobody documented.

## What is expected

- Keep criticism specific and about the work: what breaks, under what input, and
  what you would do instead.
- Take a "no" without relitigating it. Maintainer time is the scarcest resource
  here.
- When you are wrong, say so plainly and move on. Nobody is keeping score.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning or discriminatory comments, sexualised
language or imagery, publishing anyone's private information, and sustained
disruption of discussion. This applies in issues, pull requests, commit
messages, and anywhere else the project is being discussed.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through [GitHub's abuse
reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) or by opening a private
security advisory if you would rather it stay off the public tracker. Reports
are handled in confidence.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit or delete offending content, and may block accounts from
the repository. There is no appeals committee; this is a small project and the
bar is simply whether people can keep working here.
